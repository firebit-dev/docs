CrownGrab

This item only works when running on the server. Serverdormant

Keep-away minigame. Half the players start wearing a crown; anyone without one can steal a crown by touching its wearer. Whoever is wearing a crown when the timer runs out wins.

Rules

  • 2 players minimum, 120-second cap, rotates over battle_islands, construction_site and volcano_beach.
  • Crowns are handed out at construction to every other player in the round list, so roughly half the field starts crowned and the rest start hunting.
  • Wearing a crown slows you slightly (Util.defaultWalkSpeed - 2) — being crowned is a handicap, which is what keeps holders moving instead of camping.
  • Touching a crown's handle transfers it: the wearer loses it and their speed is restored, the toucher gains it. A player already holding a crown cannot steal a second, so crowns can never be concentrated.
  • Crowns are armed 0.4s after being granted, preventing an instant re-steal by whoever you just took it from.
  • A crown holder dying destroys their crown outright — it is removed from the game rather than dropped. The round therefore ends early once the last crown is gone.

The crown instance name includes a per-server random suffix (CROWN_NAME), so the mode's "is this player crowned?" check cannot be spoofed by another system naming an accessory Crown.

BaseGameMode hooks

Implements BaseGameMode Play, and also overrides Stop — see the caution on CrownGrab:Stop. Crown handout happens in the constructor rather than in Init.

Play yields on BaseGameMode:GameCountdown with a per-second condition that ends the round as soon as no crowns remain, then declares every surviving crown holder a winner.

Gated by FeatureFlags.Minigames — disabled by default; per-project forks enable it deliberately.

Round state, on top of everything inherited from BaseGameMode.

Functions​

new​

</>
CrownGrab.new(props: MinigameTypes.GameModeProps) → CrownGrab

Creates a Crown Grab round and immediately crowns every other player in the round list, so the field starts split between holders and hunters.

Also watches for leavers so a departed player does not keep a phantom crown in crownHolders and hold the round open.

Handout happens here rather than in an Init hook, which means it runs before BaseGameMode:Start has spawned anyone — players without a living character at construction time are silently skipped by CrownGrab:GiveCrown.

GiveCrown​

</>
CrownGrab:GiveCrown(
player: Player--

Who to crown

) → ()

Crowns a player: attaches the crown accessory, slows them to HOLDER_WALK_SPEED, and records them in crownHolders.

Refuses when the player is dead, has no character, or is already wearing a crown — the last check is what keeps a hunter from accumulating crowns.

The steal handler on the crown's handle is armed 0.4s later, so the player you just took it from cannot instantly take it back. When it fires, the old wearer is un-crowned and their speed restored, the crown instance is destroyed, and this method recurses to crown the thief with a fresh one.

A death listener removes the crown from crownHolders if the wearer dies, which permanently removes that crown from the round.

Play​

</>
CrownGrab:Play() → ()

The round. Called by BaseGameMode:Start and yields until the countdown ends.

After a two-second grace period it runs BaseGameMode:GameCountdown with a per-second condition that keeps the round alive only while at least one crown exists — so a round where every holder dies ends immediately rather than running out the clock with nothing to contest.

Whoever is still crowned when it ends is declared a winner. Multiple winners are normal.

Stop​

</>
CrownGrab:Stop() → ()

Cleans up this mode's own connections.

DANGER

This overrides BaseGameMode:Stop without calling it. None of the base teardown runs: winners are never paid, the round summary is never broadcast, playing is never cleared, the map is never destroyed and characters are never reloaded. Since BaseGameMode:Start finishes by calling self:Stop(), a Crown Grab round ends by cleaning one Maid and leaving everything else live.

Stop is not part of the subclass contract described on BaseGameMode; mode-specific teardown belongs on the Stopping or Destroying signals instead.
