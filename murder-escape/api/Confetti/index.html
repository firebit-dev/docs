Confetti

Fires a burst of colored confetti with sound at a point in the world, or (via Confetti.ui) inside a GUI, or (via Confetti.portal) on a GUI layer of its own that outlives the GUI it came from.

Six ParticleEmitters — one per palette color — are cloned onto an invisible anchor part, along with a one-shot pop and a looping white-noise bed. The emitters are pulsed in a loop for the requested duration rather than emitting continuously, which gives the burst a staggered, hand-thrown feel.

Confetti.Confetti(
origin: CFrame,--

Where the burst is centred

length: number?--

Seconds to keep emitting (defaults to 2)

) → BasePart--

Anchor part holding the emitters and sounds

Starts a confetti burst at origin and returns the anchor part holding it.

The part is parented to Workspace and cleans itself up: the emit loop destroys it once length elapses. Destroy the returned part yourself to cut the effect short.

This spawns and plays sound on whichever realm calls it, so calling it on the server shows the effect to everyone, and on the client only to that player.

Confetti.ui(
parent: GuiObject,--

Where the pieces live (must not clip descendants)

origin: Vector2?--

Absolute screen position of the burst

) → () → ()--

Cancels the burst

Bursts confetti inside a GUI. Pieces are plain Frames parented to a full-size container under parent, thrown in an upward fan from origin (absolute screen pixels; defaults to the centre of parent, resolved in scale so it holds before layout) and pulled down by gravity until they fade out. Plays the pop sound.

Returns a function that cuts the burst short; the container otherwise destroys itself after every piece has died.

parent must not hold a UIListLayout or UIGridLayout: the layout would place the full-size container as a cell and push its other children.

portal​

</>
Confetti.portal(
source: GuiObject,--

What the burst belongs to; its centre is the default origin

origin: Vector2?--

Absolute screen position of the burst

) → ()

Confetti.ui on a ScreenGui of its own, one DisplayOrder above the one holding source. Nothing in source's tree owns the burst, so a clipping ancestor (a ScrollingFrame) can't cut it and closing the window, switching its tab or unmounting source mid-burst leaves the pieces flying. The layer copies the source ScreenGui's inset settings, so origin (absolute pixels as source reports them) lands on the same spot, and it is destroyed after the burst. A source outside any ScreenGui (a UI Labs story) bursts in place through Confetti.ui instead.
